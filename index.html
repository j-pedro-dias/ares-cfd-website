Before your first run / Requirements

Set up the solver environment.

ARES CFD builds and runs OpenFOAM cases from Grasshopper. Install the solver environment first; add a dedicated visualizer for deeper field inspection.

Required · before using the simulation workflow

Install OpenFOAM

Install an OpenFOAM distribution before using ARES CFD’s simulation workflow, and make it reachable through WSL. The plugin includes an installation detector and runner, but it does not install OpenFOAM for you. Check that your chosen distribution launches successfully in WSL before running a case.

No single OpenFOAM release is specified by the current project. Follow the installation instructions for the distribution you use. OpenFOAM on Windows / WSL ↗
Recommended · optional companion

Install ParaView

ParaView is a useful companion for interactive 3D inspection of OpenFOAM meshes and fields. It is separate from ARES CFD and is not required to configure or run a case with the plugin.

OpenFOAM's user guide describes its ParaView readers and post-processing workflow. OpenFOAM + ParaView guide ↗
A closer look / Reef-scale flow

See the wake behind the reef.

ARES CFD can turn the analyzed velocity field into a 3D, threshold-defined wake surface, then show the downstream sampling stations, recovery distance and recirculation length in Grasshopper.

For a reef, the wake is the flow shaped downstream by the reef’s form. Its length, width, velocity deficit and recovery describe how the structure redirects flow and where slower or recirculating water persists. Those patterns matter because corals are fixed in place: currents and turbulence help govern the exchange of momentum and materials around and within the reef canopy. A wake view helps compare how geometry and flow setup change that physical setting; it is a hydrodynamic result, not a direct prediction of coral health.

Wake extentHow far the flow disturbance continues downstream.
RecoveryWhere the sampled flow approaches its reference condition.
RecirculationWhere reversed flow appears behind the geometry.
Spatial structureHow the thresholded wake occupies the domain around the reef.
Reef-canopy turbulence affects exchange of mass and momentum, while measurements show that wake-zone velocity structure can create mixing. Monismith, 2021 ↗ · Asher et al., 2019 ↗

Next / Research tools

From wake structure to reef processes.

Planned analysis and visualization tools will make more of the vertical and rotational structure of simulated flow accessible in Grasshopper.

The current source already includes an Upwelling Analysis component. Its visualization companion, together with the vorticity analysis and visualization pair, are the next areas of development.

Next · analysis

Upwelling analysis

Quantify positive vertical velocity, thresholded cell counts and upwelling fraction across simulation times.

Why it matters for reefs

Upwelling can deliver cooler, nutrient-rich water and food subsidies to some reefs, but effects vary by place and conditions. Analysis can help locate and compare these vertical-flow events.

Next · visualization

Upwelling visualization

Show where thresholded upward-flow cells occur relative to reef geometry and how the pattern changes with time.

Why it matters for reefs

Seeing the spatial footprint can help assess whether upward transport intersects reef surfaces and where monitoring or further analysis may be useful.

Next · analysis

Vorticity analysis

Calculate and summarize rotational flow structures and their strength around the reef and through the wake.

Why it matters for reefs

Vorticity helps describe flow separation, eddies and shear-driven mixing—structures linked to exchange and transport around complex reef forms.

Next · visualization

Vorticity visualization

Map where rotational structures form, persist or break down around reef geometry.

Why it matters for reefs

Putting eddy patterns in spatial context can help interpret localized mixing and potential pathways for particles and larvae, without treating the visualization as a biological forecast.

Upwelling may have mixed ecological effects: nutrient supply can be beneficial in some settings, while nutrient enrichment can increase stress in others. Interpret CFD indicators alongside site-specific environmental data. McLachlan et al., 2023 ↗ · Stuhldreier et al., 2015 ↗
